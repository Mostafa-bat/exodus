<Column alignItems="center" fullWidth={true}>
  ## <Color id="gold">Caminite</Color>
</Column>

<ItemImage id="minecraft:air" scale="0.25"/>

Caminite is a very sturdy ceramic, mixed from a bit of clay and sand. The mixture yields, when baked, a material that you believe will be hard enough for your purposes throughout all of your mechanical ambitions.

Craft <ItemLink id="caminite_blend" /> from clay and sand, then smelt it in a furnace to produce <ItemLink id="caminite_brick" />, the basic building material for most Embers machines.

Nothing in Embers is stage locked, so you can craft Caminite and build the whole machine set whenever you like. The one real gate is <Color id="red">Muspelheim</Color>, because that is the only place the Ember Bore produces anything. Building ahead of time is fine, but the machines sit idle until you have Ember to feed them.

<Column alignItems="center" fullWidth={true}>
  ## <Color id="gold">Tinker Hammer</Color>
</Column>

<ItemImage id="minecraft:air" scale="0.25"/>

<Row>
  <ItemImage id="tinker_hammer" />
  ### <Color id="aqua">Tinker Hammer</Color>
</Row>

The <ItemLink id="tinker_hammer" /> is your most-used Embers tool. It pounds two ingots of metal into a plate, and is how you persuade several machines to cooperate.

This pack rebuilds its recipe: <Color id="green">five iron ingots and two sticks</Color>, with no lead required. That makes it craftable long before you have a lead supply.

The Tinker Hammer is also used to <Color id="green">link Ember Emitters and Receptors</Color>, which is essential for building your Ember power network later on.

<Column alignItems="center" fullWidth={true}>
  ## <Color id="gold">Prospecting</Color>
</Column>

<ItemImage id="minecraft:air" scale="0.25"/>

Using some copper and iron, you have found a way to create a device capable of measuring the density of Ember energy at a position in the world.

<Row>
  <ItemImage id="atmospheric_gauge" />
  ### <Color id="aqua">Atmospheric Gauge</Color>
</Row>

The <ItemLink id="atmospheric_gauge" /> measures the Ember density at your current position. Use it to find areas rich in Ember before placing your Bore.


<ItemImage id="minecraft:air" scale="0.25"/>

The <ItemLink id="field_chart" /> gives an overview map of Ember concentrations across a wider area, helping you plan where to set up your operations.

<Column alignItems="center" fullWidth={true}>
  ## <Color id="gold">Ember Extraction</Color>
</Column>

<ItemImage id="minecraft:air" scale="0.25"/>

<Row>
  <ItemImage id="ember_bore" />
  ### <Color id="aqua">Ember Bore</Color>
</Row>

While bedrock may halt your downward advances, it will not halt your machines. The <ItemLink id="ember_bore" /> must be fueled with normal furnace fuel items, and must be placed where the blades of the Bore can reach <Color id="green">three blocks of bedrock</Color>.

<Color id="yellow">The Bore only works in <Color id="red">Muspelheim</Color>, below <Color id="gold">Y -20</Color>.</Color> This pack rewrites its output table, and Muspelheim is the only dimension left on it. Set one up anywhere else and it will burn fuel forever without producing a thing. Embers' Nether boring is switched off entirely.


<ItemImage id="minecraft:air" scale="0.25"/>

<ItemGrid>
  <ItemIcon id="ember_shard" />
  <ItemIcon id="ember_crystal" />

The Bore returns three things, and not in equal measure:

| Output | Share of rolls |
|---|---|
| <ItemLink id="ember_shard" /> | <Color id="gold">60</Color>, by far the most common |
| <ItemLink id="ember_crystal" /> | <Color id="gold">20</Color> |
| <ItemLink id="ember_grit" /> | <Color id="gold">20</Color> |

Through the immolation of the crystals and shards, you can activate the power within them. Grit is the catalyst the Catalysis Chamber runs on, so do not throw it away.

Ember Clusters are created by alchemically fusing multiple shards together for even more power. These are the raw fuel for the entire Ember energy system.
